## Abstract

We will use Landau Theory as the theoretical framework for our macroscopic dynamics, and use information theory and representation theory as the computational tool to transform the system into a useful space to look for collective excitations. 

## Introduction

I am interested in the dynamics of ideas. It is not possible to use physics to describe some abstract notion of an idea. However, the way humans interact with ideas is through physical representations of them––written and verbal language, art, music, etc. This is now in the realm of real, observable physics.

The purpose of this document is to justify the claim that statistical mechanics and information theory are the correct tools to describe the dynamics of the communication of ideas within human populations. Information theory does a good job at describing the containers of information, but does little to describe the dynamics of it. This is where statistical mechanics becomes useful. My primary claim is that the communication of ideas within a human population obeys the same dynamics as those of physical systems evolving towards thermal equilibrium. 



## Theory

### Foundations in Statistical Mechanics

Statistical Mechanics relies on the assumptions of the microcanonical ensemble. In condensed matter theory, it is common to transform the description of the physical system from its standard description in position space to some other basis. As long as there is a bijection between physical space and the space you choose you describe the system with, the same counting arguments that the microcanonical ensemble relies on hold. A simple example is Debye's theory of solids, where by transforming the description of the solid from position space to momentum space, we find collective excitations of the system known as phonons.

### Landauer's Principle

Information theory is useful for describing the information capacity of some container of information. Landauer's limit tells us that the capacity of a physical system to hold information is determined by the system's energy. Specifically, if we have a system in thermal equilibirum with a heat bath at temperature T, then it takes $\k_B T N \ln{2}$ joules to impose the state of the systems by N bits of Shannon information.   

Thus, the more energy in a system, the more information it is able to hold. In other words, the energy scale of the system also defines the information scale of the system. 

Therefore, we will assume nothing about the dynamics of the source of information - the humans who are writing, speaking, creating physical messages. We will assume nothing about their minds and make no attempt to model their minds. We will focus ourselves on the "bulletin board" that they communicate on - physical space: media, sound waves, etc. And we will consider the energy scale of this physical system as the energy required to construct that system from some reference state.
